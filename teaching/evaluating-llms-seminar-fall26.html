What is this seminar about?

Evaluating capabilities of large language models.

  • Evaluation datasets and benchmarks for LLMs
  • The design, construction, limitations, and evolution of benchmarks
  • From early static benchmarks to dynamic and human-preference evaluation
  • A critical look at benchmarks: contamination, saturation, gaming
  • Mostly text-based LLMs (other modalities are optional)

Why evaluation matters

We (they) built impressive systems that somehow do magical things. But what can they actually do?

  • How do we know, and when can we trust the numbers?
  • We need the vocabulary to discuss these questions
  • Benchmarks shape what the field builds
  • The end goal: know what to expect, build better models (and benchmarks), and encourage responsible development and deployment

The critics

I

Peer Reviewer #1 Presents

The paper is under review at a top venue (NeurIPS, ACL, ICLR, CVPR). Complete a full review using the venue’s official form: accept or reject, and what changes you would require.

You give the initial presentation of the paper, up to 10 minutes.

II

Peer Reviewer #2

Same background job, but you write a review (see e.g. the ACL review form). No presentation, but contribute to the discussion as someone who deeply understands the work.

The critics

III

Data Hacker

You're considering using the dataset to evaluate your new model, but is the data actually good? Is it high quality? Do you agree with the labels? Investigate it, label a (meaningful and representative) random subset yourself, and report agreement with the original labels.

Now what?

IV

Archaeologist

Place the paper among previous and subsequent work: Choose one older paper it cites that substantially influenced it, and one newer paper that builds on it. Present these works, explaining their relevance to the original paper, and the chronological context in which they appear.

Now what?

V

Academic Researcher

Propose a follow-up benchmark that builds on this paper’s existence and contributions. What did the original benchmark not solve? It should be harder to match today's LLMs capabilities.

Now what?

VI

Frontier Lab Employee

Pitch why your lab should invest in this evaluating this benchmark, with at least one positive and one negative real-world impact of using it. Is it easy? Would collecting data or creating synthetic one solve it?

Now what?

VII

AGI Pilled

Is the data being solved by the latest SOTA model? Evaluate the benchmark with the 2–3 best models to date: at least one open-weights and one API-based. Use a sample if evaluating the full set gets too expensive.

Gossip

VIII

Private Investigator

Run a background check on one of the authors: where they worked, what they studied, what led them here, and what motivated this paper. You may contact the authors; be courteous and on-topic.

Can you do it better?

IX

Figure 1 Designer Runs the laptop

Create an alternative Figure 1: neat, self-explanatory, nicely designed, and on point. See this guide to making a Figure 1.

Everyone sends you their slides, so the class presents from a single computer.

Got Stuck?

Research papers can be challenging to read. Here is how to get help.

AI models
  • Clarify concepts and terminology
  • Ask about specific sections or equations
  • Create a summary or video explanation
Classmates
  • Discuss confusing parts together (there are 9 of you each week!)
  • Different perspectives (roles!) clarify ideas

Write to the authors

Emailing authors is common and encouraged. They are usually happy to clarify.

When
  • Well ahead of class
  • After you have read the paper carefully
How
  • Be specific about what you don’t understand
  • Show you’ve read the paper
  • Be polite and concise
You can also consult with me: elazary@cs.biu.ac.il

Finding papers online

  • Many tools exist, each with different strengths and weaknesses
  • Check citations and references in key papers
  • Look for recent surveys and review papers
  • Follow research groups working in your area
  • Most papers are on arXiv; also check proceedings (NeurIPS, ICML, ACL…) and social media (X, Bluesky)

Search strategies

  • Use AI search tools with keywords (e.g. “benchmark contamination”) or paper titles
  • Go backwards & forwards. Find 1–2 key papers, check their references, then the papers that cite them. (The Archaeologist’s job.)
  • Filter by recency and relevance. The last 2–3 years for current methods; older foundational papers for background.

Using AI

You may, and should, use AI tools, on two conditions.

  • Declare it before your presentation, for any kind of AI use
  • Make it your own. Go over the output and understand everything you present. AI can hallucinate: verify facts, figures, and citations.
AI should help you do a better job, not replace you. You are responsible for the correctness of everything you present.
